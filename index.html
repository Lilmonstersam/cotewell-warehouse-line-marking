COLD APPLIED PLASTIC (CAP).

CAP is a two-part resin that is trowelled or extruded onto the floor at a far greater thickness than paint, then cures chemically into a hard-wearing layer bonded to the concrete. That thickness is the point: it takes the abrasion of forklift tyres scrubbing through a turn, resists oils, wash-down chemicals and hot tyre pickup, and stays legible on rough or previously patched slabs where thinner systems fail early.

It suits lines, hatching, symbols and lettering, can be laid with an anti-slip aggregate where grip matters, and is worth costing over its service life rather than per metre. Individual sections can be lifted and re-laid as the layout changes, so a single worn intersection does not force a full remark.

How much does warehouse or commercial line marking cost?

Cost depends on the area, layout complexity, surface preparation, marking material, number of colours and symbols, site access and whether installation must happen after hours. Cotewell can assess the site and provide a project-specific quote.

How long do warehouse floor line markings last?

Longevity depends on the floor condition, traffic volume, vehicle turning, cleaning methods, chemical exposure and the selected system. Tape, paint, two-pack epoxy, cold applied plastic and projected marking each suit different wear profiles.

How much downtime is needed for line marking installation?

Downtime varies with preparation, layout size and the cure or application requirements of the selected material. Work can often be staged by zone or scheduled around shifts. Tape and projected marking may suit sites where access windows are especially limited.

What information is needed to plan a line marking layout?

Useful inputs include a site plan, pedestrian and vehicle routes, loading points, storage areas, machinery footprints, exclusion zones, floor condition, cleaning regime and future layout changes. An on-site assessment can confirm measurements and operational constraints.

Can line markings support workplace safety standards and site compliance?

Clear markings can support a site's traffic management, risk controls and safety communication. The final layout should reflect the site's risk assessment, operational rules, workplace health and safety duties and any applicable Australian Standards or client specifications.
